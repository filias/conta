If you invoice **businesses in other EU countries**, you don't charge them
Portuguese IVA — the client pays VAT in their own country (*autoliquidação* /
reverse charge). In exchange, Portugal wants to know who those clients are, so
tax authorities can cross-check: that's the **declaração recapitulativa**. It
lists each EU client's VAT number and how much you invoiced them.

## Do I need to file it?

**Yes**, for every quarter in which you invoiced a service to a client that:

- is a **business** registered for VAT in **another EU country**, and
- the service is taxed there under the general B2B rule (art. 6.º, n.º 6, a)
  CIVA) — which covers most freelance work: software, design, consulting,
  translation, marketing…

This applies whether you're in the quarterly IVA regime or exempt under
art. 53.º.

**No**, if in that quarter you only invoiced:

- clients in Portugal,
- private individuals (B2C) anywhere,
- clients outside the EU (including the UK, USA, Switzerland),
- services that are VAT-exempt in the client's country (e.g. financial or
  insurance services).

No EU invoices in a quarter → nothing to file for that quarter. There's no
"zero" declaration.

## When

Quarterly, by the **20th of the month after the quarter ends** (art. 30.º RITI)
— moved to the next business day if that's a weekend or holiday:

| Quarter | Due |
| --- | --- |
| Q4 (Oct–Dec) | 20 January |
| Q1 (Jan–Mar) | 20 April |
| Q2 (Apr–Jun) | 20 July |
| Q3 (Jul–Sep) | 20 October |

> **This is a month *before* the IVA quarterly return**, not on the same day.
> It's common to do both together in February / May / September / November —
> but by then the recapitulativa is already late.

The €50,000 rule that makes some people switch to monthly filing only counts
*goods* sold to other EU countries, so if you only sell services you always
file quarterly.

## Before you start

- Every EU client's **VAT number**, validated on
  [VIES](https://ec.europa.eu/taxation_customs/vies/). Do this when you first
  invoice them — the number has to be valid for the reverse charge to apply.
- The total you invoiced **each** EU client in the quarter (the fatura-recibo
  amounts, rounded to the euro).

## Steps

1. Log in to the [Portal das Finanças](https://www.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt)
   and search for **"Declaração recapitulativa"** (or go to
   *Serviços → IVA → Declaração recapitulativa do IVA → Entregar declaração*).
2. **Quadro 01** — your name and NIF are filled in.
3. **Quadro 02** — tick **1 (1.ª declaração)** and **1B (Não)** for "change of
   frequency". Use **2 (substituição)** only to correct one you already sent.
4. **Quadro 03** — the year (campo 1) and the quarter under **Trimestral**
   (campo 3).
5. **Quadro 04** — one line per client:
    - **País de destino** and **Prefixo** — the client's country and its VAT
      prefix (e.g. Slovakia → `SK`, Germany → `DE`, Greece → `EL`).
    - **N.º de identificação fiscal** — the client's VAT number, without the
      prefix.
    - **Valor** — everything you invoiced that client in the quarter, in whole
      euros. Several invoices to the same client go in **one** line.
    - **Indicador do tipo de operação** — **5** (prestações de serviços).
6. **Quadro 05** fills itself in. Note **campo 19** — the IVA return for the
   same quarter must show the same amount in its **campo 7**.
7. Click **Validar**, fix anything it flags, then **Submeter**. Keep the
   comprovativo (PDF).

## Afterwards: the IVA return

When you file the IVA quarterly return a month later, put the same total in
**campo 7** (operations listed in the declaração recapitulativa). If the
numbers don't match, the AT will flag a divergence. If you ever correct the
recapitulativa, submit a replacement IVA return for that quarter too.

## Late or forgot one?

Submit it as soon as you notice — the portal accepts it after the deadline and
marks it as late. Missing or late declarations can be fined (art. 117.º RGIT),
but the fine is much lower if you regularise on your own before the AT notifies
you. If several quarters are involved, it's worth asking a contabilista.
